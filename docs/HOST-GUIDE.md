# StayVibe — What it does for a villa host

Written for a host evaluating the product, and structured so it can later feed
an in-app help section and a self-service bot. Every item below is **built and
running on dwarka.stayvibe360.com today** — nothing here is roadmap.

*Last updated 28 October 2026.* This guide is the overview; the **Training
Manual** inside the app (`/Training-Manual`, on your own address) explains every
screen step by step.

Four areas. Compliance leads because it is the one a host cannot solve with a
spreadsheet, and the one that carries a legal penalty.

---

## 1. Compliance — the part you legally cannot get wrong

Kerala hosts sit under the **Registration of Foreigners Act** and the **Kerala
Police Hotel Guest Rules**. Most small hosts handle this with a paper register
or a WhatsApp photo of a passport. That is the exposure StayVibe removes.

| What you get | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Digital check-in link**, one per booking channel | Guest fills it before arrival. No register, no queue at the door. |
| **Form C per foreign national** — not per booking | The law requires a separate Form C for *every* foreign guest, including children with their own passport. The form collects each one, with their own passport, visa and arrival details. |
| **Passport scan reading** | The guest photographs the passport page; the machine-readable zone is read and the fields pre-filled. They confirm — it never overwrites what they typed. |
| **Passport and visa images stored per guest** | Each scan is attached to the person it belongs to, not dumped against the booking. |
| **Indian guests take a separate track** | Aadhaar / PAN / licence instead. The two flows are kept strictly apart, so a domestic booking never ends up carrying foreign paperwork. |
| **"I'll bring originals at check-in"** | For guests who don't have documents to hand, without blocking their registration at midnight. |
| **A record of what each guest filled in** | Every registration is also written to the guest's folder in your Google Drive as a plain-text record: the people they named and their ages, date of birth, address, each foreign guest's passport and visa details, travel plans and every request. Anything a guest left blank says "(not given)", and ID, passport and visa numbers show only their last four digits, so the record can be read, and emailed to you, without a full number in it. When a guest says "I filled it in", you can check. |
| **Names and ages of the whole party** | One tap on a booking lists the people the guest named, with their ages, and says how many of the party that covers ("4 of 6 guests listed"), so a family bigger than the booking is visible before arrival. The purpose of the visit is shown with it, and beside the guest's phone number you see the flag and name of its country. |
| **A party that grows is caught before arrival** | If a guest asks Airbnb for more people (Airbnb's "wants to change their reservation" email is read for you), or gives a different head count on the check-in form, the booking is flagged and **cannot be marked ready for check-in until you approve or decline** it, with the adults and children recorded. Your caretaker sees "guest count not settled" on their own list. |
| **A night added on Airbnb is applied for you** | When a guest asks Airbnb to move their dates (a night more, say), the request is read for you too. Once you have accepted it on Airbnb and tap **I accepted it: change the dates**, the booking moves to the new dates and the extra night closes on every calendar you have linked (Airbnb, Vrbo, Agoda, Booking.com). It is refused, and says why, if another guest, a night you closed or another platform's booking already holds a night the new dates add. The amounts are yours to enter from Airbnb. If Airbnb's calendar ever disagrees with a booking and nothing is waiting for your decision, you get an email so nothing drifts. |

Your staff see, per booking, which guests still have no passport on file.

## 2. Business development — filling the calendar, and keeping more of it

| What you get | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Enquiry pipeline** | Every enquiry logged, quoted, chased and marked won or lost, with the quote ready to send on WhatsApp in a few taps. A conversion dashboard shows where they die. |
| **A kind "not available" reply** | When the dates are taken, a card offers the open dates just before and after and a ready-written WhatsApp apology. Sending it is logged on the enquiry, with a follow-up two days out, so the guest is offered an alternative instead of a silence. |
| **Rates & seasons** | Edit your nightly rate for each party size yourself, then add seasons for your busy weeks (Christmas to New Year, Guruvayur Ekadasi, Onam) and, if you like, off-season discounts: a percentage on your rates or prices of their own, each shown beside today's price, repeating every year or for one year only (for a festival whose date moves). A quote prices each night by the season it falls in, so a stay that runs into a season pays the season's price only for those nights, and the quote shows the regular price beside the season's. "Get pricing" on an enquiry (and so the WhatsApp quote built from it) and your agent links use it, and a "try it" box shows what any dates would cost. |
| **Channel calendar** | Pulls the iCal feeds from your OTA listings into one month grid, each platform in its own colour, so an Airbnb booking and a Booking.com booking on the same night are visible before they collide. It also gives each platform a private calendar link to import back, holding every night taken anywhere else (your direct bookings included) but never that platform's own bookings, so the platforms block each other's nights instead of waiting for you to notice. Airbnb and Vrbo share calendars both ways; Booking.com and Agoda only where they allow it for your listing. A booking made on a platform that reaches you only as blocked dates (Agoda, Booking.com, Vrbo) is emailed to you as soon as it appears on that platform's calendar, with the dates and a reminder to add the guest; if those nights are already taken here, the email is a double-booking warning. |
| **Held nights** | When you agree a late check-out (or an early check-in), the night it affects is closed to new bookings automatically: it shows on the calendar, the enquiry availability check says "not available", and every platform's calendar link carries it. A guest leaving at 6 PM is still in the villa when the next family would arrive at 4, so that night is not sold. A time that still leaves the villa enough hours to be made ready (your turnaround setting: at 4 hours, a check-out up to noon) closes nothing: it is only flagged on the calendar and the night stays open to book. A time a guest merely types on the check-in form closes nothing until you approve it, and you are asked first if somebody is already booked into the night. |
| **Block dates** | Close nights the villa cannot take guests (family use, maintenance, a festival) in a few taps. They show on the calendar, are refused by the availability check and go out on every platform's calendar link. It is not a booking: no guest, no commission, no revenue to explain later. |
| **Double-booking detection** | If a check-in form arrives that overlaps an existing stay, the booking is flagged for review and you are alerted — rather than discovered on arrival day. |
| **One guest, one record** | If a guest's own form makes a second record for a booking you already have (they typed their name differently), a card offers to merge them in one tap: the money stays on one stay, nothing is counted twice, and the name the booking was made under is kept. |
| **Travel agent quote links** | Send each agent or sales partner a personal link. They enter dates and guests and see their quote at once — night by night, busy seasons included, with their discount built in — without ever seeing your rate card. You see how many quotes each link has produced. |
| **Campaign tracking links** | Create a named link per campaign or channel, then see clicks → enquiries → bookings against it. You learn which WhatsApp group or listing actually produces business. |
| **"Family needing flexibility" page** | A public page where a guest with an OTA booking asks for early check-in or late check-out. It quotes them, and puts a direct-booking offer in front of a guest who is currently paying OTA commission. This is your OTA-to-direct conversion route. |
| **Guest repository** | Every past guest, their stays and their contact details, so a repeat or referral is one search away. |

## 3. Brand & reviews — the thing that compounds

| What you get | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Guest messages, pre-written, one tap** | Arrival directions, a welcome-home comfort check for a guest who has just checked in, a host introduction (which thanks a guest who has already registered), a farewell note and a review request. You tap; WhatsApp opens with the message ready. Personal, but not from scratch each time. |
| **A review asked for where the guest booked** | The farewell goes while the guest is still travelling home, and the review request follows it. Both ask for a 5-star review by name and send each guest to the right place: the Airbnb app for an Airbnb guest, your Google page for a direct guest, a note that Booking.com or Agoda will email their own link, the Vrbo app for a Vrbo guest. Each guest is pointed to the review page that matches how they booked. |
| **Review capture from Airbnb** | New reviews are picked up automatically, matched to the stay, and the rating and highlights recorded against it. |
| **Review chasing** | Guests who have left without reviewing are listed with the platform they booked through, how long ago they left and how often you have asked. One tap sends the request; the list does the remembering, so the follow-up is deliberate, not by memory. |
| **A check-in experience that looks like a business** | Your name, your villa, your branding — not a Google Form. It is often a guest's first interaction with you. |

## 4. Operational efficiency — the daily grind

| What you get | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Staff daily screen** | Your caretaker opens one link and sees today's active guest, the next five arrivals, and check-in/check-out **times** — plus a warning when a departure and an arrival share a day. A view-only **Calendar** shows every booking on every platform, the held nights and the nights you closed, so they know who is in and who is coming; it has no platform links and nothing on it can be changed. |
| **A guard against checking in the wrong guest** | After a check-in the caretaker's screen picks nobody: they tap the guest at the gate. A guest booked for a later date than today shows the date in red. One due tomorrow can be checked in a day early, but only by typing their name; one due two or more days away cannot be checked in from the caretaker's screen at all (you change the booking's date first). You are emailed when it is attempted, refused or goes ahead. A guest arriving after midnight on their booked date is fine. If a check-in was a mistake, every checked-in guest has their own **Undo check-in** button on your Complete booking page, and one checked in before their booked date is marked in red. |
| **Check-out, then "Ready to close?"** | After each check-out the app asks your caretaker whether to close the stay. They do it themselves, so you are not the bottleneck; whatever they leave open waits on their list. |
| **Cars and number plates** | Your caretaker photographs the car and number plate at the gate (or later, if it rains) and the plate number is read for them to confirm. Photos can be kept in your Google Drive, or just the plate number as text, as you prefer. |
| **Guests' own arrival and departure times** | The times a guest types on the form appear on every screen. When they ask for more than your standard times you are asked to approve; an approved time is the **agreed** one your caretaker and the WhatsApp messages then use. |
| **Breakfast the way you serve it** | A guest can ask for breakfast on the form (up to two kinds); you can record exact counts ("5 Appam and 5 Idli") and your caretaker's screens and the breakfast charge follow them. Larger parties can have a group rate you set. |
| **Booking completion & money** | Per-booking tariff, OTA fees, extra charges, occupancy tax and what you actually net. OTA fee structures are built in. Each extra charge can be ticked as received, with who and when, so you see what has been paid and what is pending. |
| **Expenses and incidentals** | Villa expenses, kitchen incidentals, breakfast and car-rental entries, logged by staff as they happen. |
| **Inventory and preferred stock** | What's in the villa, what to reorder. |
| **Staff & access** | Add staff, set what they can see, and record salary or salary-plus-commission. Check-in and check-out actions are recorded against the person who did them. |
| **No-show and refunds** | Mark a no-show and record a manual refund against the booking, so the accounts still reconcile. |
| **Your own time zone, everywhere** | Every time on screen and in your alert emails is shown at your property's local time, with the zone named, whether you open the app in Kerala or California. |
| **Notifications** | Choose what you get alerted about, and where. |
| **A training manual inside the app** | A step-by-step manual for you and your staff, with a link to every section and a print / save-as-PDF button. An accountant or a new manager can be given their own time-limited passcode. |

## What a host should expect in the soft-launch

- **This is early.** You are getting it at ₹500 against a ₹2,000/month list
  price because your feedback shapes what gets built next.
- **Your data is yours and is kept separate from other hosts'.**
- **Setup is done for you** during onboarding — property details, rates, staff,
  branding, and your check-in links.
- **What is not automated:** guest messages open WhatsApp pre-written for you to
  send — the product does not send messages on your behalf, and does not run or
  spend money on advertising.
- **Platform prices stay on the platforms.** Rates & seasons prices what you
  quote yourself: enquiries, WhatsApp quotes and agent links. What Airbnb,
  Booking.com or Agoda charge is set on each of them, and calendar links carry
  dates only, never prices.
